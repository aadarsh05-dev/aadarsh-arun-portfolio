Automated Prediction Market Bot

A bot that trades Kalshi prediction markets on its own. The goal is a strategy that still makes money after fees, and the rule is that nothing touches real money until it has earned it in testing.

I'm following Miles Deutscher's published process for building an AI trading bot and adapting it from crypto exchanges to Kalshi. Claude handles the slow thinking (designing and testing strategies), and a fast decision model makes the second-by-second calls.

The process

  1. Laptop setupPorted the starter bot to Kalshi and dry-ran it on live prices.Done
  2. Backtest engineFinding data and tools to replay past markets with real fees.Now
  3. Strategy huntTest many ideas, keep the ones that survive fees and unseen data.Next
  4. Forward testWeeks on live prices with simulated fills.Planned
  5. Always-on serverMove from my laptop to a server running 24/7.Planned
  6. Small real moneyA separate go/no-go call, with tight limits.Planned

What I've built

  • Ported an open-source crypto trading bot to Kalshi's API: request signing, hourly market rollover, Kalshi's fee formula, and paper fills that only count contracts that actually traded at my price.
  • Ran three dry runs on live Kalshi prices and checked the simulated fills against Kalshi's public trade record.
  • Tested the kill switch: it halts the bot and closes the position in 0.16 seconds.

What I've learned so far

  • The starter's risk limits don't fit prediction markets. A 3-cent move on a 50-cent contract is a 6% swing, and the first dry run hit its daily loss limit in about a minute.
  • The last 10-15 minutes before an hourly market closes are chaotic, so exiting 5 minutes before the close is too late.
  • Went through Kalshi's free historical data (14,500+ series across 20 categories) and code-reviewed two open-source backtesters. Neither passed: wrong fee model, positions never settled at $1 or $0, and real bid/ask prices ignored.

Crypto Strategy Backtest & Product Brief

A fee-aware backtest of four rule-based crypto strategies, built to answer one question honestly: does automation actually beat just holding?

  • Backtested buy-and-hold, dollar-cost averaging, a moving-average trend rule, and an RSI reversion rule against 10 years of daily BTC and ETH prices, at three fee levels.
  • Found that fees consumed 34–55% of the active strategies' pre-fee gains, and that buy-and-hold beat the automated strategies in 5 of 6 strategy-asset pairings.
  • Wrote the results up as a product brief with an MVP scope, success metrics, and a section on the regulatory limits of automated trading advice.
